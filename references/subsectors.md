# Software Subsector Frameworks

Assign companies by buyer, product function, data flow, and revenue model rather than by marketing language. For diversified companies, analyze material segments separately and disclose overlap.

## Cybersecurity

Key categories include endpoint, identity, network and cloud security, security operations, application security, data security, and governance. Important questions:

- Does the vendor control a durable enforcement point or only generate alerts?
- Is the platform replacing tools or bundling acquired products without integration?
- Does telemetry improve detection across customers?
- How much revenue depends on seats, protected workloads, data volume, or modules?

Useful indicators: ARR, NRR, module adoption, platform consolidation, large-customer growth, remaining obligations, gross margin, sales efficiency, incident history, and threat-intelligence breadth. Main risks include platform bundling, breach liability, tool fatigue, open-source substitutes, and high data-processing cost.

## Data platforms

Includes cloud databases, warehouses, lakehouse platforms, observability, data integration, governance, and developer data infrastructure. Map the product to storage, compute, query, movement, orchestration, and governance.

Useful indicators: consumption growth, committed spend, remaining obligations, customer cohorts, workload count, data processed, net expansion, cloud-provider concentration, gross margin, and optimization trends. Distinguish customer usage from recognized revenue and separate underlying workload growth from unit-price changes.

Main risks include hyperscaler competition, open-source alternatives, workload portability, customer optimization, compute cost, and architectural shifts.

## Enterprise workflow

Includes IT service management, customer service, HR, finance, collaboration, low-code automation, and other systems of record or action. Identify the economic buyer and whether adoption is departmental or enterprise-wide.

Useful indicators: seats, modules per customer, enterprise customers, NRR, renewal rate, RPO or cRPO, implementation duration, partner ecosystem, and time to value. Analyze whether AI creates new paid workflows, reduces seat demand, or is bundled into existing suites.

Main risks include suite consolidation, long deployments, shelfware, weak user adoption, seat pressure, and competition from incumbent productivity or ERP platforms.

## Advertising and transaction platforms

Includes ad-tech, commerce enablement, payments-related software, marketplaces, and platforms whose revenue depends materially on customer transaction volume. Separate software-like recurring fees from media, pass-through, incentives, and transaction revenue.

Useful indicators: ad spend or gross volume, take rate, active customers, retention, revenue excluding traffic-acquisition or pass-through costs, contribution margin, loss rates, platform concentration, and regulatory exposure. Normalize for election, holiday, macro, and channel-mix effects when relevant.

Main risks include cyclical volume, privacy changes, walled gardens, take-rate compression, fraud or credit losses, customer concentration, and regulation.

## Peer-selection rules

Prefer peers sharing most of these characteristics:

- same economic buyer and budget pool;
- similar product mission and competitive set;
- comparable pricing unit and contract duration;
- similar gross-margin and infrastructure intensity;
- similar scale, growth stage, and geography.

Do not combine high-gross-margin seat software, infrastructure-heavy consumption platforms, and transaction businesses in one unlabeled multiple ranking. If a cross-subsector comparison is necessary, normalize with growth, gross margin, free cash flow, dilution, cyclicality, and revenue visibility.

## Subsector output

For each company or basket, report:

1. subsector and product layer;
2. economic buyer and budget source;
3. primary revenue model;
4. sector-specific KPIs;
5. closest peers and why;
6. structural tailwinds and failure modes;
7. any segment mix that weakens direct comparability.
