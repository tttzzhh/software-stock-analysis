# Moat and Risks

Evaluate moats through evidence of durable customer value and difficult replacement. Do not treat market share, brand claims, or product breadth as sufficient by themselves.

## Workflow depth

Assess where the product sits in the customer's workflow:

- **System of record:** authoritative data or business state resides in the product.
- **System of action:** recurring work is executed through the product.
- **Permissions and governance:** identity, policy, audit, or compliance controls depend on it.
- **Integration depth:** critical upstream and downstream systems are connected.
- **Customization:** schemas, workflows, models, or business rules are embedded.
- **Collaboration and habit:** multiple functions rely on shared history and process.
- **Migration burden:** data, retraining, downtime, validation, and regulatory approval raise switching cost.

Look for renewal durability, expansion across modules, implementation depth, partner activity, and customer case studies with measurable outcomes. Countercheck implementation failures, downsells, declining NRR, and customers consolidating vendors.

## Self-build risk

Self-build is more plausible when the function is strategically differentiating, open-source components are mature, internal engineering talent is available, data must remain controlled, and vendor pricing exceeds lifecycle cost.

It is less plausible when requirements change rapidly, compliance and uptime are critical, integrations are broad, specialist data or threat intelligence matters, or the product benefits from learning across customers.

Compare total cost over several years: engineering, infrastructure, maintenance, security, compliance, opportunity cost, and migration. A prototype is not equivalent to a supported production system.

## Bundling risk

Bundling pressure is highest when:

- a platform vendor already controls distribution or identity;
- the feature is good enough for most customers;
- procurement wants fewer vendors;
- the standalone product lacks cross-platform differentiation;
- switching and implementation costs are modest.

A specialist can defend when it offers materially better outcomes, multi-platform neutrality, faster innovation, deeper workflows, proprietary data, superior governance, or a credible ecosystem. Track win rates against bundles, net expansion, attach rates, discounting, and changes in sales cycles.

## Commoditization and AI

Separate four layers:

1. foundation technology and infrastructure;
2. model or engine capability;
3. proprietary context, workflow, and distribution;
4. accountable business outcome.

Features at layers one and two may commoditize faster. Durable value is more likely when the vendor owns trusted context, permissions, workflow, distribution, and responsibility for the result. Test whether AI lowers cost, raises willingness to pay, improves retention, or merely adds inference expense and competitive parity.

Monitor gross margin, compute cost per outcome, pricing migration, attach rate, usage, support burden, and whether customers can reproduce the feature with general-purpose tools.

## Risk register

Use a table with:

| Risk | Mechanism | Probability | Impact | Leading indicator | Counterevidence | Time horizon |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Cover at least the material risks among:

- product displacement and bundling;
- self-build or open-source substitution;
- pricing and consumption optimization;
- customer or partner concentration;
- security, privacy, compliance, and model-governance failures;
- cloud-infrastructure dependence and gross-margin pressure;
- long implementation or weak time to value;
- acquisition integration and changing KPI definitions;
- stock-based compensation and dilution;
- macro sensitivity, procurement cycles, and foreign exchange.

Avoid false precision. Use qualitative probability and impact unless a defensible historical base rate or model exists.

## Moat conclusion

For each claimed moat, provide:

1. the mechanism;
2. observable evidence;
3. counterevidence or an alternative explanation;
4. a leading indicator that would strengthen or weaken the claim;
5. the expected persistence and why.

Conclude with **well evidenced**, **partially evidenced**, **unproven**, or **deteriorating** rather than an unexplained numeric score.
